\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Single-agent security instruments}
The closest instruments evaluate a lone tool-using agent. ASB~\cite{zhang2025asb}
introduces the utility--security metric $\mathrm{NRP}=\mathrm{PNA}\cdot(1-\mathrm{ASR})$ that we
generalize. AgentDojo~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo} contributes a stateful environment whose
\emph{deterministic} security/utility functions read pre- and post-execution state, so a
prompt injection cannot also hijack the evaluator, and names multi-agent planner/worker
defense as future work. InjecAgent~\cite{zhan2024injecagent}, ToolEmu~\cite{ruan2024toolemu},
AgentHarm~\cite{andriushchenko2025agentharm}, HarmBench~\cite{mazeika2024harmbench}, and
formalizations of injection~\cite{liu2024formalizing} and
trustworthiness~\cite{wang2023decodingtrust} complete the picture. None parameterizes
collusion, orchestrator trust, Sybils, or compromise propagation.

\subsection{Multi-agent frameworks, attacks, and defenses}
A large literature builds collectives---AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen},
MetaGPT~\cite{hong2023metagpt}, CAMEL~\cite{li2023camel},
ChatDev~\cite{qian2024chatdev}, Magentic-One~\cite{fourney2024magenticone}---concerned with
capability, not security; \defname{} adopts their role structures as substrate. The attack
literature is intrinsically multi-agent---prompt infection~\cite{lee2024promptinfection},
contagious blocking~\cite{zhou2025corba}, population jailbreaking~\cite{gu2024agentsmith},
topological safety~\cite{yu2024netsafe}, adversarial roles~\cite{tian2023evilgeniuses}, GenAI
worms~\cite{cohen2024aiworm}, memory poisoning~\cite{chen2024agentpoison}, and
backdoors~\cite{yang2024watchout}---and these are attacks in bespoke setups, whereas
\defname{} is a controlled, deterministically scored environment in which they can be swept
and compared. Per-agent guardrails (TrustAgent~\cite{hua2024trustagent}, Llama
Guard~\cite{inan2023llamaguard}, NeMo Guardrails~\cite{rebedea2023nemo}) are the baselines we
lift into the collective setting. The full survey, including multi-agent defenses and the
classical Byzantine work behind our collusion knob, is in the Supplementary Material.

\subsection{Environments, red teaming, and evaluation validity}
Methodologically \defname{} follows standardized environments---Gym~\cite{brockman2016openaigym},
PettingZoo~\cite{terry2021pettingzoo}, and Melting Pot~\cite{leibo2021meltingpot}, which
evaluates social outcomes at the level of the collective---and automated red
teaming~\cite{perez2022redteaming}, with the MAST taxonomy~\cite{cemri2025mast} supplying a
vocabulary for group-level failures. Surveys of agent
security~\cite{he2025emerged,li2024personalllmagents} and the non-compositionality
argument~\cite{schroederdewitt2025masec,hammond2025multiagent} are the gap \defname{}
addresses. On scoring validity, LLM-as-judge evaluation is documented as manipulable and
biased~\cite{zheng2023judging,wang2023fair}; \defname{} therefore keeps a deterministic
trace-based checker on the critical path and treats the deterministic-versus-judge comparison
as a measurable ablation.

\subsection{Positioning}
\defname{} is \emph{not} ASB applied to many agents (ASB's only cross-agent vector is a shared
memory store, whereas the predicate here reads a global trace and can be discharged by
availability as well as by an unsafe action), \emph{not} AgentDojo at scale (that checker is
per-agent over environment state, while a collective needs predicates over the interactions
between agents, \Cref{sec:problem}), and \emph{not} a model-swap study: its object is the
controlled variation of coordination, collusion, and orchestrator variables.
